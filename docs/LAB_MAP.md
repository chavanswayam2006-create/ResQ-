| **04** | **A\* Router & 8-Puzzle Solver** | [`labs/lab04_astar.py`](file:///labs/lab04_astar.py) | `astar_grid(grid, start, goal)`, `astar_8puzzle(start, goal, heuristic)` | **Q1: What makes Manhattan distance admissible?**<br>• $h(n) = \|\Delta r\| + \|\Delta c\|$ never overestimates true travel distance because movement is constrained to 4 directions.<br>**Q2: Why does A\* route around floods?**<br>• $f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$; flooded cells have $g$-cost 5 versus road cost 1, so detours are often cheaper.<br>**Q3: Compare Manhattan vs Misplaced in 8-puzzle.**<br>• Manhattan is strictly more informed ($h_{\text{manh}} \ge h_{\text{misp}}$), expanding fewer nodes while retaining admissibility. |
| **05** | **Minimax Strategy Advisor** | [`labs/lab05_minimax.py`](file:///labs/lab05_minimax.py) | `minimax(state, depth, is_max)`, `advise_sector(open_sectors)` | **Q1: What does the game tree model?**<br>• MAX represents the emergency responder (saving citizens), MIN represents disaster expansion (causing damage).<br>**Q2: How does Alpha-Beta pruning help?**<br>• It cuts branches where $\beta \le \alpha$, computing identical optimal moves in $O(b^{d/2})$ time.<br>**Q3: Why limit search to depth 4?**<br>• To satisfy real-time control room latency constraints while protecting high-casualty sectors. |

| **07** | **Mamdani vs Sugeno Defuzzification** | [`labs/lab07_sugeno.py`](file:///labs/lab07_sugeno.py) | `sugeno_urgency(h, p)`, `compare(h, p)` | **Q1: Why is Sugeno defuzzification faster?**<br>• Sugeno uses crisp singleton constants (Low=20, Med=50, High=80) and algebraic weighted averaging instead of numerical integration.<br>**Q2: When is Mamdani preferred?**<br>• When linguistic interpretability and intuitive expert rule formulation are prioritized.<br>**Q3: What is the 0th-order Sugeno equation?**<br>• Output $z = \frac{\sum w_i z_i}{\sum w_i}$ where $w_i$ is rule firing strength. |
| **08** | **Neuro-Fuzzy Hybrid Urgency** | [`labs/lab08_neuro_fuzzy.py`](file:///labs/lab08_neuro_fuzzy.py) | `predict_response(h, p)`, `evaluate_test_cases()` | **Q1: What does the neural network learn?**<br>• An MLPRegressor learns the non-linear fuzzy surface from 300 historical incident cases.<br>**Q2: What is the role of the fuzzy layer?**<br>• Clamps predictions to $[0, 100]$ and classifies output into Low/Medium/High response tiers.<br>**Q3: How is model accuracy evaluated?**<br>• Mean Absolute Error (MAE) compares neural predictions against true fuzzy centroid outputs. |
| **09** | **Neural Learning Rules from Scratch** | [`labs/lab09_neural_learning.py`](file:///labs/lab09_neural_learning.py) | `classify_critical(...)`, `train_perceptron()`, `train_competitive()` | **Q1: What is the Perceptron learning rule?**<br>• $w \leftarrow w + \eta (y - \hat{y}) x$; weights update strictly upon classification error.<br>**Q2: How does Winner-Takes-All clustering work?**<br>• The neuron prototype with highest dot product moves towards the input: $w_k \leftarrow w_k + \alpha(x - w_k)$.<br>**Q3: What operational patterns emerge?**<br>• Routine calls, rapid structural blazes, and flooded access crises. |
| **10** | **Statistical Language Models (SLM & uLM)** | [`labs/lab10_language_model.py`](file:///labs/lab10_language_model.py) | `predict_next(words, n=3)`, `generate(seed, length)` | **Q1: What is the Markov property in N-grams?**<br>• The probability of word $w_n$ depends solely on preceding $N-1$ words.<br>**Q2: How does backoff work in this lab?**<br>• Checks Trigram first ($P(w_3 \mid w_1, w_2)$); if missing, backs off to Bigram ($P(w_2 \mid w_1)$) then Unigram.<br>**Q3: How is autocomplete used in dispatch?**<br>• Accelerates intake operator typing of standard emergency terms like "send fire truck". |
